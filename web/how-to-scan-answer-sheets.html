How to scan answer sheets for grading

You don't need a special scanner or special paper. A school copier or a phone camera both work. This guide covers what each image needs to show, which files to upload, and how to order the pages so every student is graded in one pass.

Updated October 31, 2026

What Grade Lake looks for on each page

Every answer sheet printed from Grade Lake has a QR code in the top right that says which test and which page it is, and four black corner squares. Grade Lake finds the QR code and the corner squares to straighten the page, even when a photo was taken at a slight angle, then reads the bubbles, the name box and any written answers. So the one rule that matters most: get the whole page in the image, with all four corners showing.

Step by step

  1. Put each student's pages together

    For a test with more than one answer page, stack each student's pages together, page 1 first. Grade Lake starts a new student at every page 1, so a page out of place is counted with the wrong student and flagged.

  2. Scan or photograph every page

    On a copier, scan the stack with the document feeder. With a phone, lay each sheet on a flat, plain surface and hold the phone straight above it. Either way, show the whole page with the QR code and all four corner squares.

  3. Save as JPG or PNG

    Grade Lake reads JPG and PNG images, one image per page. It doesn't accept PDFs, so set the copier to scan to JPG. You can upload the images as they are or put them in one .zip file.

  4. Keep the files in order

    Images you choose are graded in file-name order, and a zip in the order its files are stored. Copiers and phones number files in the order they were captured, so scanning the stack in order is usually all it takes.

  5. Upload and grade

    On the Grade Sheets page (part of Grade Lake Pro, with a 7-day free trial), add the images and the test file, then choose Grade. You review names, unclear marks and written answers before exporting the scores.

Tips for clean scans

  • Light it evenly. Daylight or a bright room is best. Avoid the phone's own shadow falling across the page, and glare on glossy surfaces.
  • Keep it flat. Smooth out folds and curled corners. A bent page can throw the bubbles out of line.
  • Don't crop the corners. Turn off any automatic "document" cropping that trims the page edge, or check that it leaves the corner squares in.
  • No need for huge files. 200 to 300 dpi, or an ordinary phone photo, is plenty. Grade Lake scales each image to about 2,000 pixels on its long side before reading it.
  • Ask for dark, full marks. Students should fill each bubble completely with pencil or pen. Very light marks are shown to you to settle during review rather than guessed.
  • Scan only answer sheets. If you printed a separate answer sheet, leave the question pages out. They have no QR code, and a student whose stack includes one is set aside with a note.

If a page doesn't read

When a page can't be read, for example because the QR code is cut off or blurred, Grade Lake skips that student with the reason and still grades everyone else. The results list the skipped students. Rescan their pages and grade them in a second run.

Questions, answered

Can I use my phone instead of a scanner?

Yes. Grade Lake uses the QR code and the four black corner squares on each sheet to straighten the page, so a photo taken at a slight angle works. Keep the whole page in the frame, flat and evenly lit.

Can I upload a PDF from the copier?

Not yet. Grade Lake accepts JPG or PNG page images, or one .zip file of them. Most copiers can scan to JPG instead of PDF; choose one image per page.

What resolution should I scan at?

200 to 300 dpi is plenty. Grade Lake reduces each image to about 2,000 pixels on its long side before reading it, so very large scans don't help and only make the upload slower.

Do I scan the question pages too?

No. With a separate answer sheet, scan only the answer sheet pages. A question page has no QR code, so if one is scanned by mistake, that student's sheets are set aside with a note instead of being graded.

What happens to a page Grade Lake can't read?

That student is skipped with a reason, such as a QR code that couldn't be found, and everyone else is still graded. A faint or unclear bubble isn't skipped: it's shown to you to settle during the review. Rescan the skipped pages and grade them again.
